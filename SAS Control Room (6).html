The method

Every creative starts with a buying decision.

We find why the right customer would choose your product, make ads that communicate that reason, and let tests and commercial evidence shape the next brief.

Most tests lose. The few that win pay for all of them.

Losing tests are stopped early, while they're still cheap, and each one still teaches the next brief. Winners get the budget and keep earning. That imbalance is where the growth comes from.

01 / 07 · Understand

Understand the buying decision

We start from your data and the story behind each number. Then the buyer of this exact product: what they want, what they compare it with, what makes them hesitate, how aware they already are, and what your competitors already tell them. We come back here when the product, market or season changes.

Example · commuter bag

Commuter bag. Buyers carry a laptop to work every day and are comparing bags. Their doubt: will my laptop actually fit?

02 / 07 · Hypothesis

Write one testable reason

One reason the right customer would choose this product, written down before anything is made. Big levers come first for quick wins: product focus, audiences and unit economics. Then the message, piece by piece. It gets rewritten when a test breaks it.

Example · commuter bag

“For commuters considering this bag, showing laptop fit and everyday organization may sell it better than a general lifestyle image.”

03 / 07 · Create

Build the ad around that reason

Concept, angle, hook, copy, visual, proof, offer and call to action all serve the hypothesis. Made from your product photos, footage and brand assets, with editing and AI-assisted tools. People review every asset.

Example · commuter bag

Proof: the laptop sliding into the bag. Hook: “Fits your laptop. And your day.”

04 / 07 · Test

Ask one clear question

Each test has a clear question, a fair comparison and a measurement goal, and runs until there is enough evidence to decide.

Example · commuter bag

Laptop fit demonstration against a general lifestyle image.

05 / 07 · Measure

Judge it by the business

Attention shows how people reacted. New customers, what they cost and the contribution they leave decide whether it is worth scaling. Results are read in context: timing, spend, placement, audience and the page after the click.

Example · commuter bag

A high click rate alone doesn't win. We check whether the clicks became new customers at a cost the margin can carry.

06 / 07 · Learn

Separate a lead from proof

We compare what we planned, what actually appeared in the ad and what happened. A pattern is a lead, not proof. Missing data, thin evidence and a true zero are three different things.

Example · commuter bag

If the laptop was hard to see in the ad, the hypothesis was never really tested.

07 / 07 · Next brief

Feed the next brief

Refine what's promising and build useful variations, then go straight back to Create. If a test broke the hypothesis, we return to step 02 and write a new one.

Example · commuter bag

Next: the same idea with the laptop clearly in frame, plus a new hook on organization.
